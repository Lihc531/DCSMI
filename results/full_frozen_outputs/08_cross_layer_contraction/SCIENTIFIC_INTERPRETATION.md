# Information-compression analysis v1.0 — scientific interpretation

The observed hierarchy shows strong monotonic contraction of cross-syndrome GJSD: herb 0.5514 -> target 0.05276 -> GO-BP 0.004219 and KEGG 0.006016. Thus only 9.57% of herb-layer discrimination remains at the target layer; of target-layer discrimination, 8.00% remains in GO-BP and 11.40% in KEGG. Cumulative functional-layer discrimination is 0.77% (GO-BP) and 1.09% (KEGG) of the herb-layer value.

Exact Curveball reproduction of the pre-existing Phase12A target null is machine-consistent (sorted null max difference ~2.6e-15). Target GJSD is greater than exact degree-preserving expectation (upper-tail empirical P=0.00290), confirming that real herb-target assignments retain more syndrome differentiation than degree structure alone.

At the function layer, GO-BP GJSD does not exceed exact structural-null expectation (upper-tail P=0.0895). KEGG global GJSD does exceed the absolute function-level structural null (upper-tail P=0.0233), but the prespecified pathway-level differential analysis previously found 0/621 FDR-supported syndrome-pathway tests. Moreover, the observed target->KEGG retention ratio is within the exact structural-null retention distribution; therefore KEGG provides evidence of weak distributed global differentiation, not identifiable syndrome-specific pathway mechanisms.

Accordingly, the strict prespecified manuscript decision is PIVOT rather than GO: the monotonic contraction phenomenon is real and quantitatively large, but its topology-aware explanation is not uniform across GO and KEGG. The paper should not claim that topology alone causes all functional specificity loss. A defensible claim is that treatment-level syndrome discrimination undergoes marked projection-associated contraction; target-space differentiation remains above degree-structure expectation, while functional specificity becomes weak/diffuse and does not yield multiplicity-controlled syndrome-specific pathways or patient-level validation.
